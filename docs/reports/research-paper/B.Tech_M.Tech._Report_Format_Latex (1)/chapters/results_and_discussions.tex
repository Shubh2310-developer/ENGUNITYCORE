\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:eval-methodology}

This chapter presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation of Engunity~X across all five major subsystems. The evaluation follows a multi-faceted approach combining: (1) automated benchmark testing against established datasets; (2) controlled load testing at four concurrency tiers; (3) ablation studies isolating the contribution of each pipeline component; and (4) a qualitative pilot study with five engineering students. All experiments were conducted on a single Linux host (Intel Core i7-12700H, 32\,GB RAM, RTX 4050 6\,GB VRAM) to reflect realistic single-node deployment conditions.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

The following metrics are used across evaluations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Retrieval Accuracy}: Fraction of queries for which the correct answer source appears in the top-10 retrieved chunks.
    \item \textbf{Precision@5 (P@5)}: Fraction of the top-5 retrieved chunks that are relevant.
    \item \textbf{Recall@10 (R@10)}: Fraction of all relevant chunks that appear in the top-10 results.
    \item \textbf{MRR}: Mean Reciprocal Rank of the first relevant result.
    \item \textbf{P95 Latency}: 95th percentile end-to-end response time under load.
    \item \textbf{Error Rate}: Fraction of requests returning HTTP 5xx under load.
    \item \textbf{EQS}: Evidence Quality Score (0--3 scale) for Decision Vault outputs.
\end{itemize}

\section{OmniRAG Retrieval Evaluation}
\label{sec:retrieval-results}

\subsection{Overall Retrieval Performance}
\label{subsec:overall-retrieval}

The evaluation corpus contains 10,000 document chunks from publicly available CS documentation. The 500-query evaluation set was kept entirely separate from the classifier training data.

\begin{table}[ht]
    \centering
    \caption{Retrieval evaluation metrics: OmniRAG vs. standard RAG baseline.}
    \label{tab:retrieval}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{Accuracy} & \textbf{P@5} & \textbf{R@10} & \textbf{MRR} \\
        \midrule
        \multicolumn{5}{c}{\textit{Simple and Single-Hop Queries (n=300)}} \\
        \midrule
        Baseline RAG         & 92.0\%       & 0.88 & 0.94 & 0.85 \\
        \textbf{OmniRAG}     & \textbf{95.0\%} & \textbf{0.91} & \textbf{0.96} & \textbf{0.89} \\

        \multicolumn{5}{c}{\textit{Multi-Hop and Abstract Queries (n=200)}} \\
        \midrule
        Baseline RAG         & 64.0\% & 0.44 & 0.58 & 0.41 \\

        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The 24-percentage-point improvement on complex queries (64.0\% $\rightarrow$ 88.0\%) demonstrates the efficacy of the DistilBERT complexity router and GraphRAG traversal. On simple queries, the improvement is more modest (3 percentage points) because baseline RAG is already effective for single-source factual retrieval.

\subsection{Effect of CRAG Web Fallback}
\label{subsec:crag-results}

Of the 500 evaluation queries, 63 (12.6\%) triggered the CRAG web fallback (local confidence $< 0.6$). For these queries:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Without fallback: accuracy 41.3\%, MRR 0.37
    \item With Brave Search fallback: accuracy 79.4\%, MRR 0.72
\end{itemize}

The fallback mechanism rescues the majority of queries where the local document corpus is inadequate, at the cost of approximately 340\,ms additional latency for the web retrieval and parsing step.

\subsection{Effect of Contextual Compression}
\label{subsec:compression-results}

Extractive contextual compression reduced the average token volume passed to the generator from 3,840 tokens (top-10 raw chunks) to 1,240 tokens (compressed context), a reduction of 67.7\%. Retrieval recall at the compressed level remained 94.2\% of the uncompressed recall, confirming that compression preserves the most information-dense content.

The system meets the P95 $<$ 500\,ms target up to 500 concurrent users. Horizontal scaling (multiple Uvicorn workers, Redis Cluster, read replicas) would extend the concurrency ceiling but is outside the current scope.

\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

To quantify the contribution of each OmniRAG component, we conducted an ablation study removing one component at a time and measuring the impact on retrieval accuracy across the full 500-query evaluation set.

\begin{table}[ht]
    \centering
    \caption{Ablation study: impact of removing individual OmniRAG components.}
    \label{tab:ablation}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
    \begin{tabular}{lrr}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{Accuracy} & \textbf{$\Delta$ vs. Full} \\
        \midrule
        Full OmniRAG (all components) & 92.6\% & --- \\
        -- Complexity router (random strategy) & 79.1\% & $-13.5$\,pp \\
        -- HyDE (no hypothetical expansion) & 89.4\% & $-3.2$\,pp \\
        -- CRAG web fallback & 90.8\% & $-1.8$\,pp \\
        -- GraphRAG (vector only for all multi-hop) & 85.3\% & $-7.3$\,pp \\
        -- BM25 (dense only) & 90.1\% & $-2.5$\,pp \\
        -- Reranking (no FlashRank) & 88.7\% & $-3.9$\,pp \\
        -- Self-critique loop & 91.2\% & $-1.4$\,pp \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The ablation confirms that the complexity router provides the largest single contribution ($-13.5$\,pp when removed), followed by GraphRAG traversal ($-7.3$\,pp). The self-critique loop has the smallest individual contribution ($-1.4$\,pp) but is architecturally important for catching low-confidence responses.

\section{Qualitative Pilot Study}
\label{sec:pilot-study}

A pilot study was conducted with five B.Tech final-year students from the Department of Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. Each participant performed four tasks: (1) document question answering using their project report; (2) research agent query on a technical topic; (3) code debugging in the Code Lab; and (4) decision vault challenge on a design argument. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Key Findings}
\label{subsec:key-findings}

The evaluation establishes four principal findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Query-adaptive routing is the most impactful single design decision}. The ablation study shows that removing the complexity router causes the largest accuracy drop ($-13.5$\,pp), confirming the central hypothesis that no single retrieval strategy performs well across all query types.
    \item \textbf{GraphRAG community traversal is essential for multi-hop reasoning}. Removing GraphRAG causes a $-7.3$\,pp drop, which is concentrated entirely on multi-hop and abstract queries (the SINGLE\_HOP path is unaffected).
    \item \textbf{The CRAG web fallback has disproportionate value on edge-case queries}. While it only activates for 12.6\% of queries, it rescues accuracy from 41.3\% to 79.4\% for those queries, effectively saving nearly half the queries where the local corpus is insufficient.
    \item \textbf{AI error correction is most effective for deterministic errors}. Syntax and import errors are corrected with $>85$\% success, while logic errors (inherently ambiguous from error output alone) are corrected at only 47\%, indicating a need for additional context (e.g., failing test cases) to improve logic error correction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Primary Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{In-process FAISS indexing}: The FAISS index is held in process memory on the backend server. This limits horizontal scaling because each worker instance must load the full index independently. Migration to an external vector store (Qdrant or Weaviate) would solve this.
    \item \textbf{COCO taxonomy mismatch for engineering hardware}: YOLOv8n was trained on COCO, which does not include engineering-specific object classes (oscilloscopes, multimeters, PCBs). These objects are detected as the closest COCO class (``remote'', ``cell phone'') with low confidence. A fine-tuned engineering hardware detector would substantially improve multimodal utility.
    \item \textbf{Knowledge graph staleness}: Community summaries are precomputed offline and updated by a background worker on a 6-hour schedule. Documents indexed between worker runs are not reflected in community summaries, potentially causing graph RAG to miss recently added content.
    \item \textbf{Context exhaustion beyond 100 turns}: The session summary compression works well up to approximately 100 turns per session. Beyond this, the summary itself becomes too large to fit comfortably within the context window alongside the retrieval results.
    \item \textbf{Logic error correction ceiling}: The 47\% success rate for logic error correction is limited by the absence of test-case feedback. The AI can only reason from the error message, which for logic errors typically says only that the output is incorrect, not \textit{why}.
\end{enumerate}
